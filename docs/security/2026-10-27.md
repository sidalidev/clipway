# Security review, 27 Oct 2026

Internal review of Clipway 0.3.0. No external audit yet.

**Scope**: what changed since the review of 25 October: the relay carries texts only, and images and files go directly between the devices (docs/PROTOCOL.md, section 14), in the reference (`relay/crypto.js`, `relay/direct.js`), the Android app (`Protocol.kt`, `Direct.kt`) and the Mac app (`ClipwayCore`, `Direct.swift`, `AppModel.swift`).

**Not covered yet**: a direct transfer between a physical phone and a Mac on a real Wi-Fi, a network that isolates its clients, the OWASP MASVS-L2 grid item by item, a MobSF report.

## Relay: texts only

- The relay refuses any item in more than one part and any envelope whose `enc` is longer than 117 440 base64 characters (a 64 KiB text, padded, encrypted): `too_big`, with a message that says why. Checked in the relay suite at the exact limit and one base64 group above it, and with 2, 26 and 27 parts.
- The frame limit is 128 KiB; a larger frame closes the connection (1009). Queues are 6 MiB per chain, 256 MiB in total by default.
- The relay cannot read the type of an item, so it cannot tell a text from an offer: offers look like short texts (512 or 1 024 bytes). Nothing in the protocol leaks that a file was sent, nor its size, to the relay.
- Residual: a modified app could put up to 64 KiB of anything in a text. That is the most the relay carries per item, and it is written in the threat model.

## Direct transfer

- Keys: K_direct = HKDF(S, "clipway-direct"), then one key per transfer, K_t = HKDF(K_direct, "clipway-direct|" + transfer id). The transfer id is drawn at random for each offer and only travels inside the encrypted offer and in the preamble.
- Records: AES-256-GCM with a random 96-bit nonce each (at most 402 records per key) and an AAD that binds the transfer, the direction and the position. The three implementations agree on the `direct` block of docs/VECTORS.json; the negative cases (other direction, other position, other transfer, tampered) are refused by all three.
- The sender sends nothing before a valid get 0 under K_t, serves one proven connection per transfer, holds 8 unproven connections at most for 5 s each, and stops listening when no offer is open (2 minutes at most). Checked: another chain's key gets nothing, garbage after a valid preamble is closed, the right receiver still gets the content afterwards.
- The receiver counts bytes as they come, never accepts more than the offered size, and checks the keyed fingerprint before writing anything; a wrong fingerprint writes nothing and tells the sender "failed".
- Addresses: receivers connect only to 10/8, 172.16/12, 192.168/16 and 169.254/16 (debug builds also to loopback, and the phone's to 10.0.2.2 for the emulator). An offer naming a public address is never tried: Clipway makes no direct connection over the Internet.
- Permissions: none added on Android (INTERNET was already there; a listening socket needs nothing more). Mac: the sandbox entitlement `com.apple.security.network.server` (docs/entitlements-mac.txt) and `NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription`.

## Tests

Relay 74 tests, Android 66 JVM tests (a real transfer on the loopback interface among them), Mac core 60 XCTests, and `mac/tools/e2e.sh` (29 checks): the Mac app against the command line client, images and files both ways directly, the relay holding only short offers, and the unreachable case answered through the relay.
