# Security review, 25 Oct 2026

Internal review. No external audit yet.

**Scope**: the protocol v1 design (docs/PROTOCOL.md), the shared cryptography (`relay/crypto.js`, `android/.../Crypto.kt`, `mac/ClipwayCore`), and the three parts at version 0.1.0 (relay, Android app, Mac app): permissions, network, storage, cryptography. It ends with a run of the real apps against each other: the Mac app on a Mac, the Android app on the Android emulator (API 36), through a relay on the same Mac. Nothing is deployed.

**Not covered yet**: a physical phone (the Pixel with GrapheneOS), the OWASP MASVS-L2 grid item by item, a MobSF report, a deployed relay behind TLS. All four are due before the first release.

## Cryptography

- Construction reused from the Sonne sync chain, reviewed in October 2026: HKDF-SHA256 from a 128-bit secret, AES-256-GCM, BIP39. Only the HKDF labels change (`clipway-proof`, `clipway-enc`, `clipway-mac`, and `clipway-id` for the id hash), so a Sonne secret gives unrelated keys here.
- The three implementations agree on `docs/VECTORS.json`, byte for byte, padded head plaintexts included: node (relay suite, 51 tests), JVM (29 tests), Swift (31 tests). Negative vectors (tampered tag, wrong AAD, swapped chunks) are refused by all three.
- Random 96-bit nonces with one key per chain: at Clipway's volumes (thousands of envelopes a day) the collision risk stays far below the 2^-32 bound for 2^32 messages.
- The AAD binds each chunk to its chain, item, index and count: chunks cannot be swapped, dropped or moved between items without detection; the head's `size` and keyed `fp` are checked after reassembly (checked end to end with a 25 MiB file in 26 parts).

## Relay

- The chain id is derived from a proof sent in the subprotocol header, never in a URL. Checked against the running relay: no proof, a 31-character proof, an uppercase proof, a missing `clipway.v1` and `clipway.v2` all get 400; a well-formed but wrong proof only opens an empty, unrelated chain; `DELETE /chain` answers 400 without a valid proof and 204 otherwise; `GET /ws` without upgrade gets 426.
- The relay stores nothing on disk. Its log over the whole run held the start and stop lines and the counters, nothing else: no IP address, no proof, no chain id, no content.
- Retention is bounded (24 hours, 50 items, 100 MiB per chain, 20 000 chains, global cap) and enforced in memory.
- Docker image: distroless, no shell, non-root, works with a read-only root file system and no capabilities.

## Apps

- Android: `aapt2 dump permissions` on the release APK gives exactly the 10 permissions of docs/permissions.txt, which CI also checks on the merged manifest. Backups and device transfer disabled, cleartext traffic refused in release (debug allows loopback only). The key and the history key live in the Android Keystore (StrongBox when present); history is AES-256-GCM at rest. Notifications are hidden on the lock screen. The image provider is not exported: another app reading a received image without the clipboard's grant gets a permission denial (seen during the run).
- Android, sensitive clips: a clip marked `EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE` by another app, sent with the real Quick Settings tile, is refused ("Not sent: marked as sensitive") and nothing reaches the relay.
- Mac: App Sandbox with exactly the five entitlements of docs/entitlements-mac.txt (checked by CI), hardened runtime, no debugger entitlement in release, debug launch arguments absent from the release binary. Keychain items not synchronizable. Received files carry the quarantine attribute.
- Mac, secrets: pasteboard content marked `org.nspasteboard.ConcealedType` or `org.nspasteboard.TransientType` is not sent (checked with real pasteboard items; the menu says "Not sent: marked as secret by the app you copied from").

## End-to-end run

Mac app (Debug build), Android app (debug APK) on the emulator, relay on `127.0.0.1:7710`, first through `10.0.2.2`, then through `adb reverse` with the pairing code. Checked:

- Pairing: the Mac draws the key; the phone joins with the 12 words, then with the `clipway:` code naming another relay (the phone asks to confirm it); hellos both ways, names on both sides; renaming the phone sends a new hello.
- Mac to phone: texts with accents and emoji land in the phone's clipboard, written by Clipway (clip label checked), with "Copied from <Mac>"; an image lands in the clipboard through the provider (96 by 64 PNG read back); with Copy automatically off, items only go to history.
- Phone to Mac: the tile, Send to Mac in the text selection menu (the Mac's pasteboard carries Clipway's source marker, the phone's clipboard is untouched), and the share menu for an image (same SHA-256 on the Mac's pasteboard, PNG and TIFF) and a 12.5 MB file (same SHA-256 in ~/Downloads/Clipway, quarantined).
- Files from the Mac: 5.3 MB and 25 MiB (the maximum, 26 parts) land in Download/Clipway with the same SHA-256, with "Received <file> from <Mac>".
- The relay killed and restarted: both apps reconnect within 2 seconds, and a text copied on the Mac while it was down arrives.
- The phone offline (airplane mode): the item waits on the relay and reaches the phone's clipboard 3 seconds after the network comes back.
- Erase everything from the phone with the relay option: the relay forgets the chain, the Mac stops (4001); the phone keeps no preference, file or key, and Download/Clipway stays. From the Mac: 204 from the relay, both Keychain items and the history gone, the phone shows "Your key was erased".

## Findings and decisions

1. Replays: a relay could replay envelopes it kept. Decided: receivers drop items older than 24 h + 10 min and remember item ids for 48 h.
2. Echo: a received text written to the Mac pasteboard would be sent back. Decided: the Mac ignores the change counts of its own writes, and both apps skip an `fp` seen in the last 10 s.
3. Stale clipboard: a phone back online could get an old clipboard. Decided: only items under 10 minutes old reach the clipboard; older ones go to history.
4. Multi-device: deletion on the first foreign ack means a third device can miss items. Accepted for v1 (one Mac, one phone), documented in PROTOCOL.md 4.4.
5. No forward secrecy: accepted for v1, documented in THREAT_MODEL.md.
6. Epoch: one epoch per relay start could make a device skip items when the relay forgot an empty chain and numbered it again from 1. Fixed in the relay (a fresh epoch for a chain it sees again or erased), PROTOCOL.md 4.3 updated.
7. Mac, erased chain coming back: after an erase from the phone, a restarted Mac reconnected with the old key and kept sending into an empty chain nobody read. Fixed: the erase is remembered across restarts, and a close code that arrives after a failed receive still stops the client. `mac/tools/e2e.sh` checks both.
8. Relay address with a capital letter (`Http://`, from the keyboard) was refused by the phone. Fixed on both apps (no capitalization in the field, scheme and host lowercased).
9. A hello could be lost when the relay changed before it was stored. Both apps now say hello again after a relay change.
10. Mac: encrypted history images whose key is gone stayed on disk. They are now removed with the unreadable index (unreadable anyway).
11. Other clipboard syncs on the same device (seen with the emulator, which copies the phone's clipboard to the Mac's) carry what Clipway writes without the password managers' markers and can hand it back as a new copy. Out of Clipway's reach; documented in THREAT_MODEL.md.
12. Erase everything does not clear the system clipboard. Documented.
13. Items waiting to leave the phone live in memory: a killed process loses them (they stay "Sending" in history). Availability only; documented in the README and the changelog.
